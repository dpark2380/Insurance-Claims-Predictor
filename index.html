Insurance Claims Predictor

A frequency-severity modelling framework for motor insurance using the French MTPL dataset

Overview

This project builds a pure premium estimation framework for motor insurance using the French Motor Third-Party Liability (MTPL) dataset. The goal is to predict the expected cost of insuring a given policy by modelling two separate but related questions:

  1. Frequency: Given a policy, what is the probability that a claim occurs?
  2. Severity: Given that a claim occurs, how much will it cost?

The pure premium is the product of these two estimates:

\[\text{Pure Premium} = P(\text{claim}) \times E(\text{claim amount} \mid \text{claim occurred})\]

This two-stage approach mirrors how insurance pricing works in practice. Actuaries separate frequency and severity because the drivers of each are different — a high-risk driver (high BonusMalus) claims more often, but when a claim occurs the repair bill is not necessarily larger than a careful driver’s.

Models

Three modelling approaches of increasing complexity are benchmarked:

Model Type Interpretability
Gamma / Poisson GLM Actuarial industry standard High — coefficients map directly to pricing relativities
XGBoost Gradient boosted trees Medium — feature importance and partial dependence plots
Neural Network Feedforward network (PyTorch) Low — most flexible, least interpretable

Dataset

Source: French Motor Third-Party Liability dataset (freMTPL2freq and freMTPL2sev), publicly available via OpenML.

Size: 679,513 policies, of which 26,439 resulted in at least one claim (3.9% claim rate).

Feature Description
BonusMalus Claims history score. 50 = clean record, increases with each claim
DrivAge Age of the driver in years
VehAge Age of the vehicle in years
VehPower Engine power of the vehicle
Density Population density of the policyholder’s area
Area Geographic risk zone (A to F)
VehBrand Anonymised vehicle brand code
VehGas Fuel type (Diesel or Regular)
Region French administrative region (22 categories)
Exposure Fraction of the year the policy was active

Key Findings

  • Claim amounts are heavily right-skewed (mean 1,569 EUR vs median 1,172 EUR). Policy features are weak predictors of individual claim cost — this is a fundamental data limitation, not a modelling problem.
  • Frequency shows much clearer patterns. BonusMalus is the strongest predictor, with drivers scoring 150+ claiming at 24% vs 3% for clean-record drivers.
  • XGBoost captures the non-linear DrivAge relationship (young driver spike at 18-22) that the GLM misses entirely, with real pricing implications.
  • All three severity models produce flat predictions clustered around the mean, confirming that policy features do not explain what drives individual claim costs.
